## FAO Digital Agriculture Findings

| Theme | Relevance to platform audit |
| --- | --- |
| Food safety and postharvest handling | The platform should support workflows beyond data capture, including downstream quality and handling signals where applicable |
| Market access | Marketplace and exchange features should be connected to real user flows and not remain isolated services |
| Finance | Lending, payments, and microfinance flows should connect to farmer operations and lifecycle data |
| Supply chain management | Traceability and logistics features should be integrated end to end, not only present as standalone pages or services |
| Mobile payments and fintech | Mobile and PWA financial journeys should be production-grade and operationally coherent |
| E-advisory | Weather, agronomy, and AI recommendation surfaces should have real backend data sources and actionable workflows |
| Input quality and traceability | Traceability should connect field, inventory, and downstream marketplace/supply-chain surfaces |
| Weather-based insurance and risk services | Weather and risk modules should connect to lending and resilience workflows where implemented |
| Land administration and digital records | Location, land, and farm registration flows should support a consistent record system and auditable data handling |

## Implication for Platform Gap Analysis

The FAO material reinforces that a production-ready agriculture platform should not treat finance, weather, traceability, marketplace, and advisory functions as isolated modules. Instead, those features should be linked across farmer registration, farm records, field operations, financing, market participation, and compliance-oriented traceability workflows.

## CGAP Digital Value Chain Finance Findings

| Theme | Relevance to platform audit |
| --- | --- |
| Efficient financial transactions | Buyer payments, loan disbursements, and repayments should flow digitally and be traceable across the platform |
| Access barriers | Branchless and mobile-first finance workflows should reduce friction for rural users rather than depend on staff-heavy manual steps |
| Savings, credit, and insurance | Financial products should connect to reliable farmer data and operational histories |
| Market opportunity improvement | Digital trading, warehouse receipt, and invoice-finance style flows should connect marketplace activity to financing features |
| Data quality risk | The platform should validate and govern farmer, farm, and transaction data because poor-quality data weakens financing and underwriting workflows |
| Rural infrastructure constraints | Mobile and offline-capable journeys remain important because network and agent infrastructure can be weak in rural settings |

## Combined Domain Implication

Taken together, the FAO and CGAP material suggests that a production-ready smallholder platform should link registration, farm records, advisory, payments, lending, insurance or risk services, and market transactions into a coherent digital value-chain workflow rather than leaving those features as isolated modules or dashboards.
